# AAOIFI Sharia Standard No. 12 — Musharaka and Mudaraba (Partnership Financing)

## 1. Musharaka (Joint Venture Partnership)

### 1.1 Definition

Musharaka is a partnership contract where two or more parties contribute capital
to a joint venture and share profits according to an agreed ratio. **Losses must
be shared strictly in proportion to each partner's capital contribution** — this
is a non-negotiable Sharia requirement.

### 1.2 Types of Musharaka

#### Permanent Musharaka (Sharikat al-Milk)

- Partners contribute capital and share ownership indefinitely
- Each partner has the right to participate in management (or appoint an agent)
- Suitable for joint venture businesses and equity investments

#### Diminishing Musharaka (Musharaka Mutanaqisa)

- One partner gradually purchases the other partner's share over time
- Commonly used for **home financing**: the bank and client jointly purchase a
  property; the client buys the bank's share in installments while paying rent
  on the bank's portion
- The purchase of shares must be through **separate, independent transactions**
  at each stage — not pre-determined in the original partnership contract
- The rental rate on the bank's diminishing share must be **independently
  determined** and not structured to replicate interest payments

### 1.3 Profit and Loss Sharing

- **Profit ratios** may be freely negotiated and need not match capital ratios
  (e.g., a partner contributing 40% of capital may receive 50% of profits if
  they contribute additional expertise or management effort)
- **Loss ratios** must always match capital contribution ratios — a partner
  contributing 40% of capital bears exactly 40% of losses
- A partner **may not** guarantee another partner's capital or minimum return —
  this converts the partnership into a loan and is **non-compliant**
- A managing partner may offer a **unilateral purchase undertaking** at face
  value at the end of the term (separate from the partnership contract)

## 2. Mudaraba (Silent Partnership)

### 2.1 Definition

Mudaraba is a profit-sharing partnership where one party provides the capital
(rabb al-mal) and the other provides management expertise and labor (mudarib).
It is fundamentally different from a loan.

### 2.2 Key Rules

- The **capital provider bears all financial losses**, except in cases of
  mudarib negligence, misconduct, or breach of contract
- The **mudarib bears the loss of effort and time** if the venture fails
- Profits must be shared according to a **pre-agreed ratio** (e.g., 60:40)
- Profits **cannot** be expressed as a fixed amount or a percentage of capital —
  they must be a percentage of **actual realized profits**
- The mudarib **may not guarantee** the capital or a minimum return
- The capital provider may not interfere in day-to-day management unless
  specifically agreed in the contract

### 2.3 Restricted vs. Unrestricted Mudaraba

- **Unrestricted (Mutlaqah)**: The mudarib has full discretion in investment
  decisions within Sharia-compliant boundaries
- **Restricted (Muqayyadah)**: The capital provider specifies the type of
  business, sector, geography, or other constraints
- In restricted Mudaraba, the mudarib who invests outside the restrictions
  becomes personally liable for any resulting losses

## 3. Application to Islamic Banking Products

### 3.1 Musharaka-Based Home Financing

```
Structure: Diminishing Musharaka
─────────────────────────────────
Bank contributes 80% → Joint ownership of property
Client contributes 20% ↗

Client pays monthly:
  1. Rent on bank's 80% share (decreasing as bank's share diminishes)
  2. Purchase installment to buy a portion of bank's share

Over 15-25 years, client acquires 100% ownership.
```

**Compliance requirements**:
- The property must be jointly registered in both names
- Rental must reflect fair market rent (not reverse-engineered from interest rates)
- Each share purchase must be a separate transaction
- Insurance on the property must be Takaful, not conventional

### 3.2 Investment Accounts (Mudaraba-Based)

Islamic banks commonly structure investment savings accounts on a Mudaraba basis:
- Depositors are the rabb al-mal (capital providers)
- The bank is the mudarib (manager)
- Profits are distributed based on a pre-agreed ratio
- The bank **cannot guarantee** the deposited capital or a minimum return
- Profit equalization reserves (PER) and investment risk reserves (IRR) are
  permissible mechanisms to smooth returns, with SSB approval
