# AAOIFI Sharia Standard No. 26 — Takaful (Islamic Insurance)

## 1. Fundamental Principles

Conventional insurance is **non-compliant** with Sharia due to the presence of:

- **Gharar** (excessive uncertainty): The policyholder pays premiums without
  knowing whether they will receive any benefit; the insurer does not know the
  extent of liability at the time of contract
- **Maysir** (gambling): The insurance contract resembles a wager — the
  policyholder "bets" on a loss occurring
- **Riba** (interest): Insurance funds are typically invested in interest-bearing
  instruments

Takaful is the Sharia-compliant alternative, based on the principles of mutual
cooperation (ta'awun) and voluntary contribution (tabarru').

## 2. Takaful Operating Models

### 2.1 Wakalah Model (Agency)

- Participants appoint the Takaful operator as their agent (wakil)
- The operator manages the fund for a **fixed fee** (wakalah fee), expressed as
  a percentage of contributions
- Any surplus in the Takaful fund belongs to the **participants**, not the operator
- The operator's fee is earned regardless of the fund's performance
- Investment profits are distributed: a portion to participants, a portion as
  incentive fee to the operator (if agreed)
- **This is the AAOIFI-preferred model**

### 2.2 Mudaraba Model (Profit-Sharing)

- The Takaful operator acts as mudarib (investment manager)
- Participants' contributions form the capital pool (rabb al-mal)
- Investment profits are shared according to a pre-agreed ratio
- Underwriting surplus belongs to **participants only**
- The operator earns a share of **investment profits only** — not underwriting surplus

### 2.3 Hybrid Model (Wakalah + Mudaraba)

- Wakalah fee for underwriting management
- Mudaraba profit-sharing for investment management
- Most common in practice; permitted by AAOIFI

## 3. Sharia Requirements for Takaful

### 3.1 Segregation of Funds

The Takaful operator **must** maintain strict segregation between:
- The **Shareholders' Fund** (operator's own capital and retained earnings)
- The **Participants' Takaful Fund** (contributions and investment returns)

Commingling these funds is **non-compliant**. Each fund must have its own
financial statements, audited separately.

### 3.2 Investment Restrictions

All investments of the Takaful fund must comply with Sharia:
- **Prohibited**: Interest-bearing deposits, conventional bonds, stocks of
  companies primarily engaged in haram activities (alcohol, gambling, pork,
  conventional financial services exceeding tolerance thresholds)
- **Permitted**: Sukuk, Sharia-compliant equities, Islamic money market
  instruments, real estate, commodity investments
- **Screening threshold**: Companies with interest-bearing debt exceeding 33%
  of total assets, or non-compliant revenue exceeding 5% of total revenue,
  are excluded from the investment universe

### 3.3 Surplus Distribution

- Underwriting surplus (contributions minus claims and reserves) belongs to
  participants and must be distributed to them or retained in the fund
- The operator **may not** appropriate underwriting surplus
- Distribution methods: pro-rata refund, reduction of future contributions,
  or allocation to a participants' reserve

### 3.4 Deficit Handling

If the Takaful fund is in deficit:
- The operator **must** provide an interest-free loan (Qard Hasan) to cover
  the deficit
- This loan is repaid from future surpluses of the fund
- The operator **may not** charge interest or any fee on this loan

## 4. Re-Takaful (Islamic Reinsurance)

- Takaful operators should seek re-Takaful arrangements with other Takaful or
  re-Takaful companies
- Using conventional reinsurance is **tolerated only** when no re-Takaful
  alternative is available, and must be minimized progressively
- The SSB must approve any conventional reinsurance arrangement annually
