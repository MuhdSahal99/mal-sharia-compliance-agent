# AAOIFI Sharia Standard No. 17 — Sukuk (Islamic Securities)

## 1. Definition and Nature

Sukuk are certificates of equal value representing undivided ownership shares in
tangible assets, usufructs, services, or specific projects or investment
activities. Unlike conventional bonds, Sukuk **must not** represent a pure debt
obligation — they must be asset-backed or asset-based.

**Fundamental principle**: Sukuk holders bear the risks and rewards of ownership
in the underlying assets. Any structure that guarantees return of principal and
a fixed periodic return to Sukuk holders is **non-compliant** as it mirrors a
conventional bond and constitutes Riba.

## 2. Permissible Sukuk Structures

### 2.1 Sukuk al-Ijara (Lease-Based)

- Sukuk represent ownership in a leased asset or the right to its usufruct
- Periodic returns come from lease rental income
- Rental amounts may be fixed or variable (benchmarked to market rates)
- The underlying asset must be clearly identified, tangible, and Sharia-compliant
- The issuer (originator) may lease the asset back from Sukuk holders (sale and
  leaseback is permissible provided the initial sale is a genuine transfer)
- **Risk sharing**: Major maintenance is the obligation of Sukuk holders as owners;
  operational maintenance falls on the lessee
- At maturity, the asset may be sold back at market value or a pre-agreed price
  (provided it is through a separate, independent purchase undertaking)

### 2.2 Sukuk al-Musharaka (Partnership-Based)

- Sukuk represent ownership in a Musharaka (partnership) enterprise
- Returns are distributed based on an agreed profit-sharing ratio
- Losses must be shared proportionally to capital contribution
- **No guaranteed returns**: The managing partner cannot guarantee a minimum return
  to Sukuk holders — this would convert the partnership into a loan
- The managing partner may offer a unilateral purchase undertaking to buy the
  Sukuk holders' shares at face value at maturity (controversial but permitted
  by AAOIFI with conditions)

### 2.3 Sukuk al-Mudaraba (Investment Management)

- Sukuk holders provide capital (rabb al-mal); the issuer manages (mudarib)
- Profits are shared according to the agreed ratio
- **Losses fall entirely on the capital provider** (Sukuk holders), except in
  cases of negligence or misconduct by the mudarib
- The mudarib may not guarantee the principal

## 3. Impermissible Structures

### 3.1 Debt-Based Sukuk

Sukuk that merely repackage a debt obligation (e.g., Sukuk al-Murabaha sold
at a discount in the secondary market) cannot be traded on the secondary market
at anything other than face value. Trading debt instruments above or below face
value constitutes Riba al-Fadl.

### 3.2 Asset-Light Structures

Sukuk where the underlying assets represent less than 33% tangible assets (with
the remainder being receivables or cash) face restrictions:
- They **must be traded at face value only** (not at a premium or discount)
- If tangible assets fall below the 33% threshold, secondary market trading
  at market prices is **non-compliant**

### 3.3 Guaranteed-Return Sukuk

Any Sukuk structure where the originator or a third party guarantees:
- Return of full principal to holders, AND
- A fixed periodic coupon or return

is **non-compliant**. This structure is economically equivalent to a conventional
bond and violates the risk-sharing principle fundamental to Islamic finance.

## 4. Sukuk vs. Conventional Bonds — Comparison

| Feature              | Sukuk (Compliant)            | Bond (Non-Compliant)       |
|----------------------|------------------------------|----------------------------|
| Represents           | Asset ownership              | Debt obligation            |
| Returns              | Profit from asset/business   | Fixed/floating interest    |
| Principal guarantee  | Not permitted                | Typically guaranteed       |
| Underlying asset     | Required (tangible)          | Not required               |
| Risk sharing         | Required                     | Risk on issuer only        |
| Secondary trading    | At market price (if asset-backed) | At market price       |
