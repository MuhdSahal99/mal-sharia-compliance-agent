# AAOIFI Sharia Standard No. 8 — Murabaha (Cost-Plus Financing)

## 1. Definition and Permissibility

Murabaha is a sale contract in which the seller discloses the original cost of the
asset and adds a known, agreed-upon profit margin. In Islamic banking, Murabaha
is widely used for asset financing where the bank purchases an asset on behalf
of the client and resells it at cost plus a disclosed markup.

Murabaha is **permissible** provided it meets the following essential conditions:

1. The bank must **genuinely own** the asset before selling it to the client
2. The asset must be in the bank's **constructive or physical possession**
3. The cost and profit margin must be **clearly disclosed** to the buyer
4. The sale must involve a **real, tangible asset** — not a monetary exchange
5. The promise to purchase (wa'd) by the client is **binding on the client only**,
   not on the institution, prior to the bank acquiring ownership

## 2. Sequence of a Valid Murabaha Transaction

A valid Murabaha to the Purchase Orderer (MPO) must follow this precise sequence:

```
Step 1: Client identifies the asset and requests the bank to purchase it
Step 2: Client provides a binding promise (wa'd) to purchase from the bank
Step 3: Bank purchases the asset from the supplier (ownership transfers to bank)
Step 4: Bank takes possession (physical or constructive)
Step 5: Bank offers to sell the asset to the client at cost + disclosed profit
Step 6: Client accepts; a separate sale contract is executed
Step 7: Client pays in installments or deferred lump sum as agreed
```

**Critical rule**: Steps 3 and 4 must occur BEFORE Steps 5 and 6. If the bank
sells the asset to the client before taking ownership and possession, the
transaction is a **financing arrangement disguised as a sale** and is
**non-compliant** (bay' ma la yamluk — selling what one does not own).

## 3. Profit Margin and Pricing

### 3.1 Permissible Pricing

- The profit margin may be a fixed amount or a percentage of cost
- The total sale price (cost + margin) must be fixed at the time of contract
- The price **cannot be increased** after the contract is signed, even if the
  client delays payment
- Benchmarking the margin against conventional interest rates (e.g., LIBOR + 2%)
  for internal pricing is **tolerated** but should be disclosed to the SSB

### 3.2 Impermissible Pricing

- A variable price that changes with market interest rates after contract signing
  is **non-compliant** — it converts the sale into an interest-based arrangement
- Charging additional amounts for late payment that accrue to bank income is
  **non-compliant** (see Riba Standard No. 3)
- Compound markup on markup (rebating and re-Murabaha on the same asset without
  a genuine new transaction) is **non-compliant**

## 4. Commodity Murabaha (Tawarruq)

Organized Tawarruq involves purchasing a commodity on a Murabaha basis and
immediately selling it to a third party for cash. This is **controversial**:

- AAOIFI Sharia Standard permits it with conditions (genuine commodity, real
  market transactions, no circular arrangements)
- The commodity must be **identifiable and actually exist** at the time of sale
- Pre-arranged buyback by the original seller is **non-compliant** (bay' al-'inah)

|-----------|--------------------------------------|---------------|
| M-8.01    | Cost-plus sale with disclosed margin | COMPLIANT     |
