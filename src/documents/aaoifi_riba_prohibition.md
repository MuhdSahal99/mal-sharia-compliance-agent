# AAOIFI Sharia Standard No. 3 — Prohibition of Riba (Interest)

## 1. Scope and Definition

Riba (interest/usury) is categorically prohibited in all forms under Islamic law.
This standard applies to all financial institutions offering Islamic financial
services and covers both **Riba al-Nasiah** (interest on loans) and **Riba al-Fadl**
(excess in exchange of ribawi commodities).

### 1.1 Riba al-Nasiah (Deferment Interest)

Riba al-Nasiah refers to any predetermined, guaranteed return on a loan or debt
instrument regardless of the performance of the underlying asset or business.
A financial institution **must not** stipulate or receive any amount above the
principal in a loan contract. Any excess charged on the principal of a loan,
whether expressed as a fixed percentage or a variable rate tied to a benchmark
such as LIBOR or SOFR, constitutes Riba al-Nasiah and is strictly prohibited.

### 1.2 Riba al-Fadl (Excess in Exchange)

Riba al-Fadl arises when ribawi commodities (gold, silver, wheat, barley, dates,
salt — and their modern monetary equivalents) are exchanged in unequal quantities
or with deferred delivery. Currency exchange must be conducted on a spot basis
at the prevailing rate; deferred currency exchanges are impermissible.

## 2. Application to Modern Banking Products

### 2.1 Fixed-Return Savings Accounts

A conventional savings account that guarantees a fixed or variable interest return
on the deposited principal is **non-compliant**. The guarantee of return above
principal constitutes Riba al-Nasiah. Permissible alternatives include:

- **Mudaraba-based savings**: The depositor acts as capital provider (rabb al-mal)
  and the bank as manager (mudarib). Returns are based on an agreed
  profit-sharing ratio applied to actual profits, not a guaranteed percentage.
- **Wadiah-based savings**: Deposits are held as trust (amanah). The bank may,
  at its sole discretion, provide a non-contractual gift (hibah), but no return
  may be promised, stipulated, or implied at the time of contract.

### 2.2 Fixed-Income Bonds

Conventional bonds that pay periodic fixed or floating coupons constitute Riba.
The Islamic alternative is Sukuk, which must represent ownership in underlying
tangible assets, usufructs, or services rather than a debt obligation.

### 2.3 Late Payment Penalties

A financial institution may impose a late payment charge to deter delinquency,
but such charges **must not accrue to the institution's income**. They must be
directed to a charitable fund (Qard Hasan fund) under the oversight of the
institution's Sharia Supervisory Board.

## 3. Sharia Supervisory Board Requirements

Every Islamic financial institution must maintain an independent Sharia
Supervisory Board (SSB) comprising at least three scholars qualified in
Islamic jurisprudence (fiqh al-muamalat). The SSB must:

- Review and approve all financial products before launch
- Issue annual Sharia compliance reports
- Audit a statistically significant sample of transactions quarterly
- Maintain independence from the institution's commercial management
